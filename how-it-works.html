How the tool works

The charter generator guides users through the major parts of a governance charter in a structured sequence. It combines plain-language prompts, structured inputs, and export functionality to help teams create a usable first draft.

02

Build your charter step by step

Step 1

Work through the main sections in order: basics, purpose, scope, roles, operations, and advanced sections.

Step 2

Use guided prompts, dropdowns, and checkboxes to shape the draft.

Step 3

Load starter content if you want a practical example to edit.

Step 4

Tailor the content so it reflects the real work, decision authority, and participation model of the group.

Step 5

Export a polished Word version that can be reviewed, refined, and finalized offline.

03

What the form includes

The built-in navigation and draft status are designed to make the experience easier to follow, especially for first-time users.

Charter Basics

Define identity, scope level, sponsor, and lead.

Purpose & Outcomes

State why the group exists, what future state it is pursuing, and what success looks like.

Scope & Authority

Clarify what the group governs, what it does not govern, and how issues escalate.

Guiding Principles

Capture the values or operating principles that should shape governance decisions.

Membership & Roles

Define participation and required business and technical perspectives.

Responsibilities & Deliverables

Describe the recurring work and concrete outputs of the group.

Operating Model

Set expectations for cadence, quorum, decision-making, and administration.

Advanced Sections

Add policy, privacy, sharing, subcommittee, and version history detail when needed.

04

What you get at the end

The output is a polished Word draft that gives sponsors, reviewers, and participants something concrete to react to. It supports review and refinement. It does not replace the final approval process.
