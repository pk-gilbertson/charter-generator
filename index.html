01

Charter Basics

Define the committee identity, scope level, and leadership.

Enter the formal name of the agency, department, bureau, or program that will own or use this charter.
Provide the official title of the governance body. Use the name people will see in agendas, documents, and approvals.
Choose the governance structure that best matches how this group will operate, such as a steering committee, advisory group, or working group.
Identify the organizational level this charter covers, such as one program, one division, the full agency, or multiple agencies.
Name the senior leader who authorizes the group, supports escalation, and helps remove barriers when decisions need executive backing.
Name the person responsible for leading meetings, setting priorities, and guiding the committee’s work between sessions.
Record the date this charter becomes active or is intended to take effect.
State how long the charter remains in effect and how often it should be reviewed, renewed, or revised.

02

Purpose, Vision & Outcomes

State why the group exists and what success should look like.

Explain why the group exists and what business need, governance challenge, or coordination problem it is meant to address.
Describe the future state the group is working toward and the mission that guides its ongoing work.
List the key goals the committee is expected to achieve. Each objective should describe a meaningful governance outcome.
Define how the agency will know the committee is effective. Use measurable indicators when possible.

03

Scope & Authority

Clarify what the group governs, what it does not govern, and how issues escalate.

List the data, decisions, processes, systems, or governance activities this group is responsible for overseeing.
List the activities or decisions this group does not own so boundaries stay clear and duplication is avoided.
Describe what the group can approve directly, what it can recommend, and where its authority begins and ends.
Explain where unresolved issues go next, including the leader or body that receives escalated decisions, risks, or conflicts.

04

Guiding Principles

Capture the beliefs or operating principles that should shape governance decisions.

List the core values or operating principles that should shape governance decisions, behavior, and tradeoffs.

05

Membership & Roles

Define who participates and which business and technical perspectives must be represented.

List the people or roles that participate in the committee. Include title, committee role, and voting status when relevant.
Identify the business, technical, legal, privacy, security, and operational perspectives that should be represented.
Define the key committee roles so members understand their responsibilities, authority, and expected level of participation.

06

Responsibilities & Deliverables

Describe the work the committee does and the products it should produce.

List the standing duties the committee is accountable for performing on a recurring basis.
Capture the major governance priorities, initiatives, or deliverables the group should focus on in the near term.
List the concrete outputs the group is expected to produce, such as standards, templates, decision logs, or reports.

07

Operating Model

Set how the committee meets, makes decisions, and maintains governance records.

State how often the group meets and, if useful, include the normal timing pattern.
Define the minimum participation needed for the group to conduct business or make formal decisions.
Describe how the group makes decisions, such as consensus, majority vote, advisory recommendation, or executive approval.
Explain who handles agendas, minutes, action items, decision tracking, and recordkeeping for the group.

08

Advanced Sections

Use these sections when your agency needs more formal governance, legal alignment, or subcommittee structures.

Policy, privacy, security, and sharing
List the laws, regulations, statewide policies, enterprise standards, or agency rules the committee should align to.
Describe when privacy, security, legal, or release review must be involved because of risk, sensitivity, or compliance requirements.
Explain how the committee will support or review internal sharing, external sharing, access requests, or data use agreements.
Working groups and subcommittees
List any standing workgroups or subcommittees that support the main body with focused work.
Version history
Track major revisions to the charter so reviewers can see what changed, when it changed, and who made the update.
